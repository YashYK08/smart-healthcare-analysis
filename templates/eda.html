Fig 1: Disease Prevalence by Age Group
Prevalence (%)
Key Observation: No chronic conditions are recorded in patients below age 45. The 46-60 group represents the clinical onset stage (14.6% diabetes, 7.8% heart disease), while patients aged 61-80 present a massive prevalence surge: 89.4% heart disease, 100% diabetes, and 79.1% stroke.
Fig 2: Calibrated Health Risk Score Distribution
KDE Histogram
Key Observation: The calibrated score resolves the constant 100.0 raw metric by engineering a 0-100 clinical index (Mean: 44.55, Std: 20.82, Range: 3.2 - 94.4). The cohort stratifies into Low (43.5%), Moderate (26.2%), High (17.3%), and Critical (13.0%) risk tiers.
Fig 3: Biomarker & Disease Correlation Matrix
Pearson r
Key Observation: Chronic conditions show exceptional collinearity: Heart Disease and Diabetes correlate at r = 0.86, and Heart Disease and Stroke correlate at r = 0.84. Age is the strongest primary predictor of disease emergence (r = 0.75 - 0.82).
Fig 4: Smoking & Physical Activity vs Heart Disease
Cross-tabulation
Key Observation: Active smokers with sedentary exercise patterns face the highest incidence of adverse cardiovascular events. Transitioning from sedentary to active lifestyle reduces disease burden across both smoking and non-smoking sub-cohorts.
Fig 5: Multi-Morbidity Triad Distribution
Cohort Counts
Key Observation: 3,215 patients (64.3%) present with zero chronic conditions; 289 have isolated disease, 289 suffer from dual morbidity, and 1,207 (24.1%) exhibit the complete triad of Heart Disease, Diabetes, and Stroke simultaneously.
Fig 6: Blood Pressure vs Fasting Glucose Trajectories
Risk Tier Clusters
Key Observation: Critical and High-risk patients cluster heavily above the clinical thresholds of 140 mmHg systolic blood pressure and 126 mg/dL fasting plasma glucose, validating the biological coherence of our risk stratification model.
Fig 7: Symptom Presentation vs Chronic Disease Burden
Symptom Rates
Key Observation: Chest pain and fatigue report rates remain consistently around 50% across cohorts. However, the combination of multiple symptoms within patients having elevated biomarkers strongly correlates with higher risk tiers.
Fig 8: BMI vs Total Cholesterol Mapped to Risk Score
Continuous Gradient
Key Observation: High BMI (≥ 30.0) combined with hypercholesterolemia (≥ 240 mg/dL) shifts patients into the upper 75th percentile of calibrated risk, illustrating compound metabolic syndrome danger.
